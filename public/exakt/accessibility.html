EXAKT — Accessibility

Last updated: 5 November 2026

EXAKT is meant to be playable by everyone. This page says what the game does for accessibility, what it does not do yet, and how to tell us when something gets in your way.

Playing without a swipe

  • With a screen reader (VoiceOver on iPhone and iPad, TalkBack on Android), the board offers each direction as an action, such as "Swipe up: the +1 waiting below comes in". Choose one from the board's actions and the move is played, just as a swipe would play it.
  • The four tiles waiting round the board are buttons that play the swipe bringing them in. They work with a screen reader, Switch Control, Voice Control and a pointer. A tile whose swipe would move nothing is faint, and its button is off.
  • Swipe buttons: turn them on in Settings, and four full-size buttons under the board play one swipe each.
  • With a keyboard, the arrow keys play moves while the board has focus. Tab moves between the buttons.
  • Placing a tile with a card: each empty square is offered as a button, named by its row and column.

What a screen reader hears

  • The board says your number, the target and the moves left. Then it names every tile with its row and column, and says which tiles cannot apply to your number: the ones the board draws hatched.
  • After each move, the board says the new position.
  • On the first three levels, the tutorial, a pop-up says each move in words before you make it, and a screen reader reads it as it comes up. The guide acts each move out as well. The mascot beside the words is a picture only, and a screen reader passes over it.
  • On levels 4 to 25, the first time two tiles merge on your board, your number takes a merged tile, or a tile burns (a ÷2 on an odd number, or one that would pass the level's ceiling or go below 0), the same pop-up says why, once, and a screen reader reads it as it comes up. While it is up, no move plays, by a swipe or any of the ways above, so nothing changes under it. Got it closes it, and No more tips turns them all off. The Show tips again switch in Settings turns them off or on again, and a screen reader reads it as a switch, on or off.
  • Every control has a label, in English or German, as the app is set.

Seeing and reading

  • Colour is never the only signal. Every tile shows its symbol and its value, and a tile that cannot apply is hatched, not only greyed.
  • Text meets the WCAG 2.1 AA contrast levels: 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text.
  • Text grows with your device's text size. It is checked up to 200% on the smallest phone, in English and German.
  • Buttons are at least 44 points high on iPhone and iPad, and 48 dp on Android. The one exception is the tiles waiting round the board, which take their taps in the room beside it, smaller at large text sizes: Settings, Swipe buttons gives every swipe a full-size button.

Motion and sound

  • With Reduce Motion (iOS) or Remove animations (Android) turned on, the game drops its animations: the board shows where a move ends without sliding there, and the mascot stays still.
  • No sound is the only signal of anything. Sound, music and vibration each have their own switch in Settings.

What is not there yet

  • Rooms, the multiplayer races, run against a clock when they open. A screen reader can play a room the same way, but the clock does not wait.
  • The board is one image to a screen reader, not a grid of squares to explore one by one. Its tiles are read out as a list instead.
  • The game has not yet been tested with people who use screen readers or switches every day. We would welcome your report.

Tell us

Write to service@kernelarch.com with what got in your way, on which device, and with which setting turned on. We read every message, and a fix goes into the next update where we can make one.
