EXAKT — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

EXAKT keeps as little about you as it can. It starts by asking when you were born, and keeps only the age group the answer gives, on the phone. A child's phone sends our game server nothing but, once, the erasure of an account it had before it became a child's: Children and teens, below, says what that means. Every other player has an account identifier, and our server keeps your game balances against it: coins, lives and a few counters. If you play in a room, it also holds that room - its numbered seats and their scores - for as long as the room lasts, and no longer. If you sign in, Firebase Authentication, which runs our accounts, also holds the email address and name your sign-in provider gives; our own server never sees them. Ads are shown through Google AdMob, which collects information from your device to show and measure them, and asks for your consent first where the law requires it. Your ads are personalised only if you say yes. If you use Game Center or Google Play Games, your leaderboard scores go to Apple or Google, never to us. We run no analytics and no crash reporting of our own; AdMob measures its ads and collects diagnostics about its own software, as Advertising below explains.

EXAKT is made by KernelArch Labs, which decides how this data is used. You can delete your account, and everything our server holds about you, at any time from inside the app: Settings → Account → Delete account.

What stays on your device

Your progress — which levels you have cleared, and your best move count and best score on each — and your settings are kept in your device's own storage, so the game works without a network. So are:

  • your lives, and any change to them or to your coins not yet sent to our server, each marked with the account it belongs to;
  • a copy of your last known coin balance, signed so it cannot be edited. It is only a copy: the real figure is on our server;
  • which account the save belongs to;
  • whether the account this phone had is in use, or is being erased because Google Play's age signal, or your answer to the age question asked again, made the phone a child's, which of the two, and whether the app has told you so;
  • the age group your answer to the first question puts you in — a child, a teen or an adult — and never the date you gave, with the age below which a player was a child on this phone when you answered, so that the app can tell when that age has changed since. It stays on this phone, and the app sends it nowhere;
  • on Android, the age group Google Play's age signal last put you in, so that the more careful of it and your answer applies: each time the app starts, it asks Google Play for your age range, which Play may first ask you to share, and keeps only the group, never the range, with the age below which a player was a child on this phone then, sending it nowhere;
  • if you are a teen, the day you told the app, before a purchase, that a parent or guardian agrees to your buying, and your age group then, so that it asks only once. It stays on this phone, and the app sends it nowhere;
  • whether you own No Ads, how many levels you have cleared since the last ad between levels, and when you last bought something, because ads pause for a day after a purchase;
  • your answer about personalised ads, yes or no, given to the app's question or with the Personalised ads switch: when you gave it, and which version of the question you have answered, as the record of what you chose. It stays on this phone, and the app sends it nowhere;
  • the price the store last gave for the smallest pack of coins, so that what coins are worth in money still shows beside each coin price when the store can't be reached;
  • two notes for the checks below: how far the device's clock is from our server's, and since when the device has been unable to vouch for itself, if it has;
  • your latest daily puzzle result: the day, your moves and stars, the swipes themselves, so that the result can be sent again if it did not reach our server, and where it stood among the day's players;
  • the medals your daily puzzles have won, if you are 18 or over: for each day you solved, the stars your first clear earned. They are never sent to us;
  • if the game shows you its story, which of its scenes you have seen, so that each plays by itself only once;
  • the numbers your album holds, which are never sent to us: the squares, primes and other special numbers you have made in a level or the daily puzzle, and which of them a result has still to show you;
  • if you are a teen or an adult, when the app last asked the store to show its rating prompt: the day, never the time, so that it asks rarely, at most once in 120 days, after you clear a level for the first time with three stars or finish a world. Whether the prompt appears is the store's choice, and any rating or review you give in it goes to the store alone: the app never learns it, and gives nothing for one. It stays on this phone, and the app sends it nowhere;
  • the number of the newest notice from us about which versions may still be played, so that an older notice, sent again by someone else, is ignored;
  • the version of the terms of service you agreed to, the day you agreed, and the age group you were in then, so that the app knows which terms you have accepted and can ask you again when they change. It stays on this phone, and the app sends it nowhere;
  • which of our notices that the game is closing, or that its terms are changing, the app has shown you, so that each is shown once;
  • if you are 18 or over and turn reminders on, the reminders waiting to be shown. There are two kinds, each off until you turn it on: Lives reminders, a notification for when your lives are full, offered when you run out of lives and in Settings; and Day-away reminders, turned on only in Settings, a notification about that day's daily puzzle or your next level, a day after you last used the app, then after 2, 4 and 7 days. None comes between 9 pm and 9 am. They are scheduled on this phone, the phone asks your permission before the first, and nothing about them is sent anywhere. Turn either off with its switch in Settings. There are no reminders for anyone under 18.

What the game saves on your device is encrypted, apart from the iCloud copy of your progress described below. It is compressed, sealed with AES-256 and kept in a small database, under a key that your device's own secure storage holds — the Keychain on iPhone and iPad, the Android Keystore on Android — and never beside the data. A copy of the app's files cannot be read without that key. Two small things sit outside it, in the app's ordinary preferences, because the device's checks need them there. On iPhone, those are the identifier of the key App Attest made for your device, its attestation until our server has it, and whether it has, under names that include your account identifier. On Android, they are the keys that seal the save and sign the balance copy, themselves sealed by the Android Keystore.

Your own cloud account, not ours

So that you do not lose your progress when you change phones, your progress can also be copied into your own Apple or Google account:

  • On iPhone and iPad, through iCloud key-value storage, if you have iCloud enabled. Apple is the data processor. The app doesn't encrypt this copy, because it has to open on a new device that doesn't hold your key. So it holds your progress and nothing else — never your coins, lives, settings or account — and iOS keeps a copy of it on the device too.
  • On Android, through Android Auto Backup, which is part of the operating system. Google is the data processor. The backed-up save stays encrypted; the key that opens it on your new phone is kept by Google's Block Store, end-to-end encrypted where your phone supports it.

An encrypted or iCloud backup of your iPhone or iPad brings the encrypted save back together with its key when you restore a new device. A backup without its key — an unencrypted computer backup, for example — brings the save back unreadable, and the game starts that device afresh; coins and lives are unaffected, because they are kept by our server.

We cannot see this data and we cannot retrieve it. It lives under your account, subject to Apple's or Google's privacy policy, and you can remove it from your account's storage settings.

Your account

Once the first question is answered, the app creates an anonymous account with Google Firebase Authentication, unless the answer makes this a child's phone, which has no account. That account holds no name, email or contact details — only an identifier — and it is what our server keeps your coins and lives against.

Signing in with Google or Apple is optional, and the game is complete without it. If you sign in with Google, Google gives us an account identifier, your email address and your display name. If you sign in with Apple, Apple gives us an account identifier and nothing else: the app asks Apple for neither your name nor your email. Firebase Authentication stores these as our processor. Signing in keeps the same account, so nothing you had is lost, and it lets your coins and lives follow you to a new phone. We use these details only to know that an account is yours. We do not email you, we do not profile you, and we do not sell or share them.

Our server

Our game server runs on Cloudflare. Against your account identifier it keeps: your coin balance; your lives and when the next one returns; when any unlimited lives you have started end, and the bought hours you have not started yet; how many coins ads have given you today, and for 8 days which videos paid you, so that none pays twice; how many of today's clears gave their life back without a device check; how many store test purchases it has credited to your account, and the coins and hours of unlimited lives they brought, which a paid purchase never adds to; one-way fingerprints of the store purchases it has credited, so that no purchase is paid twice; the identifiers of your last 128 requests and of the attempts still open, so that nothing sent twice counts twice; on iPhone, the public key Apple's App Attest issued for your device, and a counter; and, for an account that never signed in, about when it was last used, so that it can be erased after 24 months unused. It keeps no history of how you played or of what you bought, and it does not store your IP address against your account. The record is encrypted where it is stored. Each time the app syncs with it, it also says which build of the app is asking, so that a build with a serious fault can be told to update; that number is checked and not kept.

Cloudflare receives each request, including the IP address it came from, in order to deliver it to our server.

Purchases

Every level is free. The things sold are packs of coins, which buy hints and the other helpers inside a level, an hour of unlimited lives, and the removal of ads between levels. Payment is handled entirely by Apple's App Store or Google Play — we never see your payment details. After you pay, the app sends our server the signed purchase record the store gives it. The server checks the store's signature, credits the coins and keeps only a one-way fingerprint of the transaction. Each purchase carries a code derived from your account identifier, so that it cannot be claimed by another account; the code does not reveal the identifier. Beside your record, the server also notes which account redeemed each purchase, as a one-way hash of the account identifier, so that every purchase is credited once, to one account.

On Android, once our server has credited a purchase, it also confirms the purchase to Google, so that it is completed even when the app cannot do so itself. When either store refunds a purchase, it tells our server, which counts refunds by product and by reason. A purchase Apple refunded is also marked as refunded, beside the one-way hash of the account that redeemed it, so that it can't be credited again. Nothing else is kept about whose refunds they were, and once that account is deleted, the mark names nobody.

You can also buy No Ads, alone or with 60 coins. It turns off the ads between levels for good; watching a video for coins stays optional. It is a one-time purchase that the store keeps: the app remembers it on your device and asks the store again when you reinstall or press Restore purchases. For the version with coins, our server credits the coins once, the same way as for a pack.

You can also buy an hour of unlimited lives. Our server keeps it until you start it, then records when it ends, the same way it counts your lives, and every attempt until then is free.

Advertising

EXAKT shows ads through Google AdMob, including optional videos that reward you with coins. To choose, show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, AdMob collects information from your device: its advertising identifier and, on Android, its app set ID, IP address, device and app information, how you interact with ads, and diagnostic information about how its ads and its software perform, such as crash and loading data. Google processes this under its own privacy policy, at policies.google.com/privacy, and may use it for personalised ads where you allow it. How Google uses information from the apps that use its services is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites, and how it handles the data of the businesses that use its ads is at business.safety.google/privacy.

On a child's phone the app never starts AdMob or asks for an ad. For a teen, every ad request carries Google's teen setting, or in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland its child setting, as below, so the ads are never personalised and nothing is used for remarketing. It also asks Google for its restricted data processing, under which Google handles the request's data only as our service provider. iOS never asks a teen about tracking. For everyone, every ad must be one rated for general audiences.

In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before any ad is requested if you are an adult, and shows only non-personalised ads if you do not give it. A teen there is not asked: the app tells Google that the player is under the age of consent, so Google shows no personalised ads, and wherever Google's consent platform says the GDPR applies, every request of a teen's carries Google's child setting rather than its teen setting, which also keeps the request from other ad companies, such as those that measure ads. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy options. In the US states whose privacy laws let you opt out of the sale or sharing of your data for advertising, the app offers that choice too, in the same place. On iPhone, a short explanation comes before iOS asks whether to allow tracking; AdMob uses the advertising identifier only if you allow it, and you can change that in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Wherever Google's consent form doesn't apply, your ads are not personalised until you say yes. If you are an adult, the app asks you once, before any ad is personalised, with Yes and No side by side, and you play the same whichever you choose. The question says what Google gets and why, how to change your answer, and how to complain in India. Until you say yes, and whenever your answer is no, every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads and for its restricted data processing, under which Google handles the request's data only as our service provider. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → Personalised ads: turning it on asks the question again, and turning it off applies from the next ad. Wherever you are, Personalised ads turned off asks the same of every ad request.

When you watch a rewarded video, the app gives AdMob your account identifier so that Google can tell our server which account the reward is for. Nothing else about your account is shared with advertisers.

Checks that keep the game fair

So that coins and lives cannot be forged, the app asks Google Play Integrity, on Android, or Apple App Attest, on iPhone, to confirm that it is the genuine EXAKT app on a real device. Google and Apple carry out these checks. On Android, our server receives Google's signed verdict. On iPhone, it receives Apple's attestation of a key made for your device, once, and then a signature from that key with each check; it keeps the key and a counter. Before the key is made, it gives your iPhone a one-time code to sign into it, and keeps a one-way hash of that code, which works once, within a day. It keeps nothing else.

Leaderboards

If you are signed in to Game Center on iPhone or iPad, or to Google Play Games on Android, the game sends your leaderboard scores to Apple or Google: one total for all the levels you have cleared, and one for each stage of fifty levels. They show those scores on their leaderboards beside your Game Center or Play Games nickname and picture, where other players can see them. Google Play Games also collects information it needs to run its service, such as which games you play. Apple and Google do this under their own privacy policies.

The leaderboards' scores never reach our server, and neither do your nickname or your Game Center or Play Games identity. The scores are worked out on your device from the levels you have cleared. To keep your scores off the leaderboards, sign out of Game Center in iOS Settings, or out of Google Play Games in the Play Games app; the game plays the same without them.

Rooms

Rooms let up to eight players play the same levels against one clock. While a room lasts, and no longer, our server holds its six-character code and its seats. Each seat is known by an identifier derived from the player's account - never the account identifier itself, a name or an email address - and the server holds whether each seat's phone is connected, the round's levels and clock, and each seat's cleared levels, points and when it last scored. For each level you clear, the app sends the moves you played, and the server replays them to score them; it keeps the score, not the moves. If the host removes a seat before the round, the room also keeps that seat's identifier, so the same account can't come back in - again only for as long as the room lasts. A room deletes itself a few minutes after its round, and everything in it goes with it. A room takes nothing from your account: it is free, and takes no coins. While you have rooms open, your account notes the codes of the ones you opened, two at most, so that one account can't keep more open at once. The codes of rooms that have ended are dropped the next time you open a room.

The other players in a room see only numbered seats - "Player 1" to "Player 8" - whether each is connected, and each seat's points. A room has no chat and no free text of any kind, and nobody can join one without the code its host shares.

The daily puzzle

The daily puzzle is one level a day, the same for everyone. When you solve it, your phone sends our server the day and the swipes you played, with your account's sign-in token. The server checks the token, replays the swipes to see that they solve the day's puzzle, and adds one to how many players solved it in that number of moves. So that each account counts once a day, it keeps a mark made from your account identifier with a key made for that day alone, and how many moves your first clear of the day took: the mark can't be turned back into the identifier, and another clear of the same day from your account is not counted again. It keeps nothing else that says it was you: not your address, and not the swipes - apart from what every request leaves in our logs, as Logs below says. Each day's counts, its marks and its key are deleted a week after the day has ended everywhere, and then nothing can match a mark to anyone. Deleting your account doesn't delete a mark already made: once your account is gone, nobody can match it to you, and it goes with that day's counts. Anyone can read a day's counts, and they say nothing about any player. On a child's phone, nothing is sent: the puzzle is played on the phone alone. If you are 18 or over, the medal a day's clear wins stays on your device and is never sent.

The version check

When it starts, the app also downloads a small static file saying which versions are still supported, so that a seriously broken release can be stopped, and carrying, when we have one, our notice that the game is closing or that its terms are changing. That request carries no identifier beyond the IP address any web request carries: the server that hosts the file sees that address, uses it only to send the file and keep the site secure, and nothing in the request ties it to your account.

Logs

Our game server's logs are kept for 180 days, in India, as India's CERT-In directions require, and then deleted. From 13 May 2027 they are kept for a year, as India's Digital Personal Data Protection Rules will then require. For each request the server answers, and each task it runs on its own, they hold the time, what was asked, how it ended and any error, with the details Cloudflare records of a request, which may include the IP address it came from. They also hold the lines the server writes, mostly counts and outcomes that name nobody. A few can hold an account's identifier: the erasure of an account whose player wrote to ask for it, and AdMob's report of a rewarded video, whose web address carries it. Firebase Authentication's logs of our accounts are kept the same way. Only KernelArch Labs reads them. Google stores them and Cloudflare writes them, as our processors, and we give them to nobody else unless the law requires it, as it can when CERT-In asks for them after an incident. Deleting your account does not delete them: what they hold about it goes when they reach that age.

So that no one can flood it, our server also counts the requests from each IP address, and from each account, for 60 seconds at a time. Cloudflare keeps each count, and drops it when its minute is up.

If you write to us

If you email us - to ask for something, to complain, or to report an ad or a bug - we receive your address and whatever you send, and use them only to answer you and to keep a record of what was done. We keep a message for a year after the matter it raised is closed, then delete it. Report bug, in the menu, only shows details for you to copy: nothing is sent unless you send it yourself. A child's phone shows the same address, and a parent or guardian is welcome to write for a child. A message from a child is answered once and then deleted, rather than kept for the year.

Who processes your data

  • Google — Firebase Authentication (accounts), Google Cloud (our logs), AdMob (ads), Google Play (purchases), Play Age Signals (your age range, on Android), Play Integrity (device checks) and Google Play Games (leaderboards).
  • Apple — the App Store (purchases), App Attest (device checks), iCloud (your own progress backup) and Game Center (leaderboards).
  • Cloudflare — hosting for our game server.

These companies may process data outside your country: Google's services in the United States and its data centres around the world, with our logs kept in India; our game server on Cloudflare's network, in data centres around the world; and Apple's services in the United States and around the world. Where data leaves the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, their terms with us include the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, or the UK's and Switzerland's versions of them, and Google and Cloudflare are also certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Each of them is bound, by its terms with us, to protect the data it handles for EXAKT at least as well as this policy does; where one uses data for itself, as Google does for ads, its own privacy policy applies too. We share your personal information with nobody except as described here, and nobody pays us for it. But US state privacy laws count letting an ad network use a device's data to show ads as "selling" or "sharing" it, even when the ads are not personalised, and EXAKT's ads do that. Where those laws give you the right to opt out, the app offers it in Settings → Privacy options.

In Japan, sending your data to these companies abroad rests on their terms with us for handling it: each is bound by them to protect it as Japan's privacy law, the APPI, would require of us (Art. 28). It does not rest on consent, which a teen of 13 to 15 in Japan, who plays with an account, would need a parent to give. Your data goes to the countries named above, and to KernelArch Labs in India. On request, we tell you without delay how each recipient protects it and how we make sure it does, and the other things the APPI's Art. 32 lists. Those include our address and the name of our representative, which this policy will give too once the company's registered details are settled. What the app sends from your phone, to whom and why, is set out in Japanese at www.kernelarch.com/exakt/external-transmission-ja.html, and in the app under Settings → Legal, on a phone set to Japan or to Japanese.

Why we may use it

Under the GDPR and the UK GDPR, each use above rests on one of these:

  • The game you asked for (a contract with you): your account, its balances, purchases, the daily puzzle and rooms. For a teen whose contract the law where they live may not recognise, our legitimate interest in running the game they chose to play.
  • Our legitimate interests in keeping the game fair and secure, and in answering you: the device checks, the request counts, the logs, and the emails you send us.
  • Your consent, which you can withdraw at any time: personalised ads (Settings → Personalised ads, or Settings → Privacy options where Google's consent form applies) and, on iPhone, tracking (Settings → Privacy options), and reminders (Settings → Lives reminders and Day-away reminders).

How long we keep it

Your account and its record on our server are kept until you delete them, or until the phone becomes a child's, as Children and teens below says, with one exception: if you never signed in, the record is erased, with its coins and lives, once the account has gone unused for 24 months. Playing even once in that time keeps it. So does anything bought: an account you bought coins or hours of unlimited lives on is kept, however long unused, for as long as it holds coins or bought hours. Deleting your account removes the Firebase account and the server record for good. What stays is the one-way fingerprint of each purchase you made, marked as already redeemed, and as refunded if Apple refunded it, with nothing that links it to you, so that the same payment can never be credited again - on another account, or on this one. For 66 minutes after a deletion, the server also keeps when it happened, under an identifier derived from the account, so that a sign-in token from before it, which lasts up to an hour, can't be used; then that goes too. Our logs keep what they hold about the account until they are deleted, at 180 days old (a year old, from 13 May 2027), as Logs above explains. A room deletes itself a few minutes after its round, as Rooms above explains. What AdMob collects is kept under Google's own retention rules. Your progress on the device stays until you delete the app.

Children and teens

EXAKT is for everyone, children included, so the first thing it asks is when you were born: a month and a year, with nothing chosen for you. It keeps only the age group the answer gives, never the date, on the phone, and sends it nowhere. Skipping the question counts as a child's answer. On Android, Google Play's age signal can put a player in a younger group, never an older one.

If the age rules for this phone change after you answer, the app asks again, once. Beside your age group it keeps the age below which a player was a child on this phone when you answered. Where that age is older now - because a later version of the app makes a player a child up to an older age where you are, or because the phone's languages or time zone have come to say a country whose law does - and your answer could now be a younger group's, the app asks when you were born again, before anything else, with a line saying why. It asks nothing of a child, whose group can't get younger, nor of an adult, who is past every age these laws give. The new answer, too, can only put you in a younger group, never an older one; only the group is kept, never the date; and your levels stay as they are. Skipping it counts as a child's answer, as the first time. The app takes the country only from the phone's languages and, for India, its clock, never from the SIM or the phone's location.

A child - anyone under 13; under 14 in South Korea, Canada, Chile and Peru; or under 18 in India, Colombia, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa - plays on the phone alone, and we keep nothing about a child. A child's phone has no account. It sends nothing to our game server, so we hold no record of it, except once on a phone that had an account before it became a child's: it erases that account, as Parents below says. The app never starts AdMob, its consent form or the tracking prompt on it, so it shows no ads. It sells nothing, and has no sign-in, rooms or leaderboards. Its daily puzzle is played on the phone alone, and it has no reminders. It never asks the store for a rating. The only requests it makes are the version check and, on Android, the request for Google Play's age signal, besides that erasure. The version check carries nothing but the IP address any web request carries, which the file's host uses only to send the file and keep the site secure - support for the app's own working, in the words of the US children's privacy rule - and never to identify or contact the child. On iPhone, its progress is copied to the phone's own iCloud if iCloud is on, as for every player.

A teen - 13 to 17, or 14 to 17 in South Korea, Canada, Chile and Peru; in India, Colombia, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa everyone under 18 is a child - plays with an account, as an adult does, with three differences. Every ad request carries Google's teen setting, or in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland its child setting, and asks for its restricted data processing, so the ads are never personalised and Google acts only as our service provider; iOS never asks a teen about tracking; and a teen has no reminders. A teen can buy through the App Store or Google Play, where a parent can require approval first, with Ask to Buy or Google Play's purchase approvals.

Before a teen's first purchase, the app asks for a parent or guardian to agree. It keeps on this phone the day the teen said one does, with the age group then, so that it asks only once, and sends it nowhere.

EXAKT has no chat and no user-generated content. Its rooms are joined only with a code shared by the room's host, and show other players only as numbered seats with their points. Its leaderboards are Game Center's and Google Play Games', and follow the age and privacy settings of those services.

Parents: a child's phone says "On this phone only" in Settings, and "Age limit" where the child taps something the phone leaves out, such as a card that costs coins. Nothing of it is on our server, so there is nothing for us to show you or delete. A teen's account can be deleted in Settings → Account → Delete account, or without the app, as Your rights says. If a child writes to us, we answer once and then delete the message, unless a parent or guardian writes too. Questions about a child's or a teen's data are welcome at the address below.

If a phone that had an account becomes a child's, because Google Play's age signal says the player is a child, or the player's answer does when the question is asked again, the phone plays alone from then on, and the app erases that account: first its record on our server, with its coins and lives, then its Firebase account, then the copy of its balance on the phone. Offline, it waits until the phone is online. Coins bought on the account can't come to a child's phone: the app says so once, and a refund is asked of the store that took the payment, Google Play at play.google.com or Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com. If the account was signed in with Google, Google asks you to confirm before its Firebase account, with the email address and name Google gave, is erased: the app's message offers that once, and Settings until it is done. If Google Play only asks for the age to be verified, the account is kept, unused, until it is: a child's age then erases it. Skipping the question asked again erases nothing either: it says no age, so the phone plays alone and the account is kept, unused.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, the UK GDPR, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, the CCPA and comparable laws, you have rights to access, correct, delete and receive a copy of the personal data we hold about you, and to withdraw consent. Where we rely on our legitimate interests, you can object, and you can ask us to restrict what we do while a request is settled. We answer within a month. Deletion is built into the app — Settings → Account → Delete account — and needs no email. Without the app, an account signed in with Google can be deleted by email from that Google address, as www.kernelarch.com/exakt/delete-account.html explains; one that never signed in holds no name or email that could show a message is yours, so it is deleted in the app. A copy of what our server holds about your account is in the app too: Settings → Copy of my data. For anything else, or a correction, write to the address below. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

In Australia, we follow the Australian Privacy Principles. To see the personal information we hold about you, use Settings → Copy of my data, and write to us for anything it doesn't show. To correct any of it, write to the address below. Who else handles it, and in which countries, is under Who processes your data: Google and Apple in the United States and around the world, Cloudflare around the world, and our logs in India. To complain about how we handle your personal information, write to the address below: we acknowledge a complaint within 48 hours, with a reference number, and answer it within a month. If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, at oaic.gov.au.
